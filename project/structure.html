Inside an Atom

How Is an Atom Structured?

An atom consists of a small, dense nucleus surrounded by a region occupied by electrons. Although the nucleus contains almost all of the atom's mass, it occupies only a tiny fraction of the atom's total volume.

The nucleus contains positively charged protons and, in most atoms, electrically neutral neutrons. Negatively charged electrons exist in regions called orbitals around the nucleus.

Electrons do not travel around the nucleus in fixed circular paths like planets. Instead, quantum mechanics describes their locations using probability distributions.

The Fundamental Components

Meet the Subatomic Particles

Three types of particles are essential for understanding ordinary atomic structure.

+

Protons

Protons carry a positive electric charge and are located inside the atomic nucleus.

The number of protons determines the identity of the element.

Relative charge: +1

N

Neutrons

Neutrons have no electric charge and are found in the nuclei of most atoms.

Different numbers of neutrons create different isotopes of the same element.

Relative charge: 0

−

Electrons

Electrons have a negative electric charge and occupy orbitals around the nucleus.

Their arrangement influences how atoms interact and form chemical bonds.

Relative charge: −1

Identifying Elements

Atomic Number and Mass Number

Atomic Number

The atomic number is the number of protons in an atom's nucleus. Every element has its own unique atomic number.

For example, carbon has six protons, so its atomic number is 6.

Mass Number

The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons in an atom's nucleus.

A carbon atom with six protons and six neutrons has a mass number of 12.

When Atoms Change

Isotopes and Ions

Atoms of the same element can differ in their numbers of neutrons or electrons.

What Are Isotopes?

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have different numbers of neutrons.

Carbon-12 and carbon-14 both contain six protons, but carbon-12 has six neutrons while carbon-14 has eight.

What Are Ions?

An ion is an atom or group of atoms with a net electric charge.

When a neutral atom loses electrons, it becomes positively charged. When it gains electrons, it becomes negatively charged.
